# Research and Decision Rubric

Use this when a task depends on comparing sources, approaches, tools, or products. Skip it when a direct answer or simple action is sufficient.

## 1. Evidence quality

For each important claim, record:

- Claim:
- Source and publication/update date:
- Source type: Primary / Secondary / Community / User-reported
- Directly inspected? Yes / No
- What the source actually supports:
- Limitations or conflicts:

Prefer official documentation, source code, standards, direct measurements, and maintainers' statements. Community reports can reveal practical issues but should not be treated as definitive proof.

## 2. Confidence labels

- **Verified:** directly supported by inspected evidence.
- **Reported:** attributed to a source or user, not independently confirmed.
- **Inference:** follows reasonably from the evidence.
- **Assumption:** adopted temporarily to make progress.
- **Unknown:** evidence is insufficient.

## 5. Stop condition

Stop when the important decision factors are covered well enough to choose responsibly, additional research is unlikely to change the outcome, or a named evidence gap blocks progress. State remaining unknowns instead of researching indefinitely.
